# Day 59 — Learnings

## 1. A checklist framework can create an illusion of progress
Each day's prompt is well-structured and thorough — but running four of them back-to-back without ever closing the loop on one shared dependency produced four polished-looking logs describing zero shipped functionality. Structure isn't the same as forward motion.

## 2. The fix for "stuck" is smaller than it feels
After four days of blocked status, the actual next action is a single terminal command that takes under two minutes to run. Recognizing that the blocker is small — even when the accumulated logs around it feel large — is itself a useful debugging instinct.

## 3. Consistency in logging matters more than volume
Four consecutive honest "still blocked" entries are more valuable, as a real record, than skipping ahead to fabricate Day 9/10 content that doesn't reflect reality. A recruiter or future collaborator reading this repo's history gets an accurate picture of the process, including the stall — which is itself a demonstration of engineering discipline.
